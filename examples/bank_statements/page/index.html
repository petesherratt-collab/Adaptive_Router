Bank statements, worked example

Statements to Spreadsheet

How three PDF bank statements and a banking-app export become one spreadsheet that adds itself up and checks itself. Every step is ordinary code, so the same statements always give the same answer.

3 statement PDFs1 app CSV→397 transactions→template inputs→summaries and checks

All data here is synthetic. Alex Morgan, Riverside Building Society, the employer, landlord and billers are invented; the shape of the finances is copied from a real two-account setup.

Stage 1 · Read the PDFs

Every printed balance is recomputed

Each line is placed in its column by position. Dates carry down to the lines below, and shop names are joined to their payments.

Every balance printed on the statements is rebuilt from the opening balance plus each transaction. All three statements reconcile, and each opens where the last one closed.

The extraction also matches the generator's own list on all 170 lines: date, amount and description.

Stage 2 · Combine the accounts

Money moved between your own accounts is not spending

"Transfer to A Morgan" on the current account is paired with "MORGAN ALEX" arriving in the spending account: same amount, within three days. Both copies are marked internal, so the £50 is not counted twice. Savings-pot moves are marked the same way.

Stage 3 · Categorise

One category per merchant, from a short list of rules

The first rule whose words appear in the merchant's name decides. All 25 merchants matched a rule. Rules are a starting point: in the spreadsheet every merchant's category is a drop-down you can change, and every total follows.

What testing with known answers found

Balances can all agree while names go missing

Reconciling balances proves the amounts are right. It cannot prove the shop names are right, or that no day was dropped. Because this data is generated, the true answer is known, and comparing against it found two bugs the balance check had passed.

Shop names lost. When a statement prints the day's balance on the same line as the shop name, the reader kept the balance and dropped the name: 35 of 170 rows. It now keeps both.

First-day purchases dropped. The combined period started at the current account's first transaction (13 Aug), losing two spending-account purchases on 12 Aug, £17.08. It now uses the statement period.

The fixes do not change the result for the real statements this was built on: their output is byte-for-byte the same.
