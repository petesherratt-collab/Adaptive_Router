# Fast-path findings, 2026-11-25

This is the claim that the evidence from 15 to 25 November 2026 supports, and its
limits. Every number below comes from a frozen run. Plans, raw observations and
SHA-256 hashes are in the files named, and in `BUILD_HISTORY.md`.

## The claim

**When the same kind of job repeats, answering it with code that was adopted by
checking it against answers several models agreed on is much faster than asking
models each time, and at least as accurate. This holds on calculation-heavy jobs,
once a job type has repeated enough for a program to be adopted. It does not hold on
jobs that need many separate readings of text: there the program is more accurate,
but slower.**

Three rules held throughout:

- **No model judges.** Code is adopted only if, when run, it reproduces answers that
  the racing models agreed on.
- **The uncertainty flag works.** When the models reading a text disagree, the answer
  is flagged. Unflagged answers were right 94% to 99% of the time; flagged ones much
  less often.
- **Results are frozen in advance.** Every run's readings were fixed, and its files
  hashed, before its first provider call. Results on reused jobs are labelled
  descriptive; only runs on unseen jobs count as prospective.

**Real data:**
- **Monzo transaction categories:** 94.9% right with the hybrid, against 84.6% for

## Limits

- **Repetition is the price of the speed.** A program is adopted only after the race
  has produced agreed answers to check it against. When jobs arrive one at a time,
  that takes 2 to 4 jobs. Back to back it took 11 to 22, because writing and checking
  a program takes longer than a dozen jobs take to arrive. A job type seen only a
  handful of times never pays off.
- **Reading-heavy jobs are slower.** No variant tried made the program faster than the
  race on jobs with many readings. The variants were batching, narrower races,
  majority readings, a flag fallback, and racing alongside. The trade is accuracy
  (+3 to +7 points on the V6 jobs) for 1.1 to 1.4 times the time.
- **Checking is not proof.** Adoption means the program reproduces the agreed
  examples. One adopted function was wrong on an edge case no example covered
  (Timing V2). Edge-case probes were added after that, and since then no adopted
  program has been seen to be wrong. That is evidence, not a guarantee.
- **The test jobs were built by the experimenter.** The shapes were designed before
  their data, and the prose was written by a model outside the race and checked by
  hand. Real repeated tasks are the stronger test; change-signals is the first that
  runs daily.
- **Five racing models, one provider route (OpenRouter).** Rate limits (274 errors in
  Phase C) and model changes affect timing.

## What did not work

- **Models planning the steps:** worst on every comparison after the first.
- **Batching readings into one call, and racing fewer models:** cheaper, but less
  accurate.
- **Re-deciding flagged answers, by majority or by a second race:** slower, and no more
  accurate.
- **Writing the program on first sight (`write_early`):** not faster in Phase C, and it
  failed to adopt for one shape.
- **Waiting for two models to agree, without a program:** about 1 point more accurate,
  which is not significant, at 1.24 times the time.

## Open

- **Longer back-to-back streams (answered 2026-11-25).** Over 50 jobs per shape the router
  took 0.66 of the race's time. Its accuracy interval touched the −2 point bound, so the
  frozen test was not passed. Every program answer was right.
- **Can programs be adopted sooner when jobs arrive back to back?** More workers did
  not help (fast adoption V1). The delay is the end-to-end time to collect agreed
  examples and write and probe a program, about 20 to 30 s. It varies from run to run
  (11 to 21 jobs on the same jobs).
- **On real data:** how much of change-signals' cosmetic-or-not step becomes code. Read
  on 2027-01-05.
